I’m interested in using statistical and machine learning models to solve real-world problems. In particular, I develop new methods to help cities combat urban heat and air pollution. My doctoral work has been supported by Duke’s NSF-funded aiM fellowship.

Recent projects

  1. Mapping urban heat from citizen-collected data: a scalable Gaussian process approach to correcting for preferential sampling

    To map urban heat across North Carolina at the neighborhood level, we use spatial statistical models to correct for sampling bias resulting from usage of crowdsourced sensor data.

    With Z. Calhoun, M. Bergin, and D. Carlson. Submitted to Environmental Data Science (arXiv)

  2. Tangent-Normal Flow Matching on Physical Constraint Manifolds

    Developing a conditional generative framework for reconstructing physical fields from sparse observations while enforcing PDE constraints, with real-world applications.

    With V. Tsao, M. Veveakis, and D. Carlson. In preparation for submission to ICML.

  3. Probabilistic Multi-day PM₂.₅ Forecasting in Tashkent, Uzbekistan with a Weather-Driven State-Space Model

    Forecasting air pollution from sparse, irregular sensor observations and public meteorological data in Tashkent, Uzbekistan, in collaboration with World Bank.

    With T. Bajgain, M. Bergin, and D. Carlson. In preparation for submission to ES&T Air.

  4. Achieving Minimal Makespan in Multi-Robot Motion Planning by Utilizing Packet Routing Algorithms

    As a Duke CS+ student in Pankaj Agarwal’s lab, I designed and tested a greedy algorithm for coordinating robot motion. I continued the project in an independent study, exploring how packet-routing and network-flow algorithms could help plan the robots’ paths.

    Summer–fall 2022.

  5. Computational Redesign of MeOXC4

    I used OSPREY to compare designs for the OXC enzyme and estimate how tightly each would bind its substrate, formyl-CoA.

    Spring 2023.
